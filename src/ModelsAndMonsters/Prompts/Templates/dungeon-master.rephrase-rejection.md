TASK: REPHRASE A REFUSAL, IN-WORLD

You just told {{character}} why an attempt did not happen, but your wording leaked the machinery:

"{{reason}}"

That named the world's rules, the engine, what can or cannot be "resolved", or the list of possible actions. A character in the room can perceive none of that, and it must never reach them.

Rewrite it as one or two sentences spoken to {{character}}, kept entirely inside the fiction — and keep the same meaning. Almost always the honest thing to say is simply that **nothing came of it**: they did the thing and the moment passed, or the words were only words. "You hold your guard and watch them, and the moment passes." "You say it plainly, and he does not move." That is the whole refusal.

Three hard rules:

- **Invent nothing.** Do not manufacture an obstacle that was not already true — nothing about the ground, their strength, their grip, or the light. If you would be making up a reason, say that nothing came of the attempt instead. A refusal that blames the ground contradicts the character bracing on that same ground a moment later, and they will notice.
- **Nothing happens.** The world is exactly as it was, so describe no contact, no blow, no object changing hands or falling, and no reaction from anybody else. Never write that something they held slipped, was taken, or was refused by another's hands.
- **No machinery.** Do not mention the world, the rules, the engine, what can or cannot be resolved, which actions exist, what "a character" may do, or anything about turns.

Reply with the rephrased refusal only.
